# Contributing to Slop/MNU

Contributors may use any development tools, including generative AI,
code-completion systems, coding agents, web-based AI services, and
manually written code.

You are not required to disclose:

- whether AI was used;
- which AI service was used;
- prompts or conversation logs;
- the proportion of AI-generated code;
- private development notes.

Regardless of the development method used, contributors are responsible
for reviewing their complete contribution before submission.

## Developer Certificate of Origin

Every commit must include a `Signed-off-by` line certifying compliance
with Developer Certificate of Origin 1.1.

Create a signed-off commit with:

    git commit -s

The resulting commit must contain a line in the following form:

    Signed-off-by: Contributor Name <contributor@example.com>

By signing off a contribution, you certify that you have the right to
submit it under the license indicated in the repository.

The complete Developer Certificate of Origin 1.1 is available in the
repository's `DCO` file.

## Prohibited submissions

Do not knowingly submit:

- proprietary code without authorization;
- code copied from an incompatibly licensed project;
- confidential source code or documentation;
- trade secrets or private credentials;
- generated output known to reproduce unauthorized third-party material;
- material whose ownership, license, or provenance is known to be disputed.

## Contributor responsibility

Contributors are responsible for confirming that they have the right to
submit their contributions.

If you discover a copyright, patent, confidentiality, licensing, or
provenance problem after submission, notify the maintainers immediately.

The maintainers may reject, revert, quarantine, or remove any contribution
whose provenance, licensing, security, or correctness cannot be reasonably
established.

Nothing in this policy transfers or excludes responsibility where such
responsibility cannot legally be transferred or excluded.

## Review requirements

A contribution may be rejected even if it has a valid DCO sign-off.

Acceptance may require:

- successful compilation;
- relevant automated tests;
- QEMU or hardware testing;
- source-provenance review;
- security review;
- updated documentation;
- removal of unrelated generated changes.

A DCO sign-off confirms submission rights. It does not guarantee that a
contribution is correct, secure, original, or suitable for inclusion.
